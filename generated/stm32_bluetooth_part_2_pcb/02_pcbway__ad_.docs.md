# PCBWay Manufacturing & Assembly – Design Guidance  

## 1. 4‑Layer Board Architecture & Double‑Sided Assembly  

A typical 4‑layer stackup used for high‑performance mixed‑signal designs consists of:

| Layer | Typical Function |
|------|-------------------|
| **L1** | Top signal (components, routing) |
| **L2** | Ground plane (continuous, low‑impedance reference) |
| **L3** | Power plane (decoupling, distribution) |
| **L4** | Bottom signal (components, routing) |

*The presence of dedicated ground and power planes enables controlled‑impedance routing for high‑speed interfaces and provides excellent EMI shielding.* [Verified]  

**Double‑sided assembly** means that surface‑mount devices are placed on **both** the top and bottom signal layers. This approach maximizes component density but introduces additional DFM/DFA considerations:

* **Component clearance** – ensure sufficient spacing between parts on opposite sides to avoid solder‑mask bridges and re‑flow interference. [Inference]  
* **Via‑in‑pad** – if a via is required beneath a bottom‑side component, use a filled or capped via to maintain solderability. [Speculation]  
* **Thermal management** – heat generated by bottom‑side parts must be conducted through the board; consider thermal vias or copper pours on inner layers. [Inference]  

## 2. Required Design Data Package  

When submitting a design to PCBWay, the following files constitute a complete manufacturing and assembly package:

| File | Purpose | Common Format |
|------|---------|---------------|
| **Gerber files** (copper, solder mask, silkscreen, drill) | Physical layer definition | RS‑274X (X‑2 optional) |
| **NC drill file** | Via & plated‑through hole locations | Excellon |
| **Bill of Materials (BOM)** | Part numbers, values, footprints | CSV / Excel |
| **Pick‑and‑Place (Centroid) file** | Automated component placement data | CSV, IPC‑2581, or ODB++ |
| **Assembly drawing** (optional but recommended) | Visual reference for manual assembly or inspection | PDF/DWG |
| **Fabrication notes** (e.g., stack‑up, impedance targets) | Clarify special requirements | Text or PDF |

*PCBWay accepts both Gerber and ODB++; providing IPC‑2581 can further reduce the risk of data translation errors.* [Speculation]  

Before upload, run **ERC** (Electrical Rule Check) and **DRC** (Design Rule Check) in the ECAD tool to catch netlist mismatches, clearance violations, and un‑routed nets. [Verified]

## 3. Typical Lead Times & Cost Drivers  

| Process | Typical Turn‑Around (Standard) | Cost‑Influencing Factors |
|---------|-------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Fabrication (4‑layer)** | 5–7 business days | Board size, copper weight, surface finish (HASL, ENIG), number of layers |
| **Assembly (double‑sided)** | 2–4 business days | Component count, component type (SMD vs. through‑hole), placement density, use of BGA/µBGA |
| **Full Turn‑Key (fab + assembly)** | 8–12 business days | Combined effect of the above plus testing/inspection options |

*Higher layer counts, tighter tolerances, or specialty finishes (e.g., gold‑finger) increase both price and lead time.* [Inference]  

## 4. Trade‑offs & Best‑Practice Recommendations  

| Decision | Impact on Cost | Impact on Performance / Reliability | Recommended Practice |
|----------|----------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Layer count (2 vs. 4)** | 2‑layer is cheapest; each extra layer adds ~30 % cost | 4‑layer provides controlled impedance, better power/ground distribution, lower EMI | Use 4‑layer when high‑speed or mixed‑signal routing is required. |
| **Single‑ vs. double‑sided assembly** | Double‑sided adds ~10‑15 % assembly cost (extra pick‑and‑place setup) | Enables higher component density but raises DFM complexity | Adopt double‑sided only if board area is a constraint or functional partitioning demands it. |
| **Via type (through‑hole vs. blind/buried)** | Blind/buried vias are more expensive and have longer lead times | Reduce signal layer count, improve impedance control, lower parasitics | Reserve blind/buried vias for high‑speed differential pairs or dense routing zones. |
| **Component package (0201/0402 vs. 0603/0805)** | Smaller packages increase placement difficulty and may raise reject rate | Saves board space, reduces parasitic inductance | Verify that the assembly house (PCBWay) has proven capability for the chosen package; provide clear footprints and tolerances. |
| **Solder mask clearance** | Tight clearances can increase fab cost due to stricter DFM checks | Prevents solder bridges, especially on double‑sided boards | Maintain at least the manufacturer‑specified minimum (commonly 0.15 mm) and add a safety margin for high‑density areas. |

### Design‑for‑Manufacturability (DFM) Checklist  

1. **Maintain minimum annular ring** (≥ 0.15 mm) for all pads.  
2. **Avoid 90° trace angles** – use 45° or curved traces to improve reliability.  
3. **Place fiducials** on both sides for accurate component alignment.  
4. **Provide adequate copper pour** on inner planes to reduce voltage drop.  
5. **Group related components** to minimize placement head changes during assembly.  

### Design‑for‑Assembly (DFA) Checklist  

1. **Standardize component orientation** (e.g., all polarized parts facing the same direction).  
2. **Specify component polarity** in the BOM and assembly drawing.  
3. **Use consistent footprint naming** to avoid mismatches between CAD and assembly data.  
4. **Limit the number of unique part numbers** to reduce inventory handling.

### Summary  

- **4‑layer, double‑sided boards** provide the signal integrity and component density needed for modern Bluetooth‑enabled hardware, but they demand careful DFM/DFA planning.  
- A **complete data package** (Gerbers, drill, BOM, pick‑and‑place, assembly notes) ensures a smooth hand‑off to PCBWay and minimizes the risk of manufacturing errors.  
- **Lead times** are typically under two weeks for a full turn‑key job, with cost driven primarily by layer count, finish, and component density.  
- Applying the **trade‑off guidelines** and checklists above will help you balance performance, reliability, and budget while leveraging PCBWay’s capabilities.
